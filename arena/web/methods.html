Methods

What is measured, how it is aggregated, what is refused outright, and what a published number is a claim about. Read this before reading the leaderboard; a rank without these definitions is just a sort order.

1. What a published number is a claim about

A leaderboard row is often read as a single statement — system A is better than system B — and that reading is almost always too strong. What a row here actually asserts is narrower:

Under Season S, with runtime R, skill revision K and source snapshots D, system A produced a validated artifact on n cases, and these eight dimension scores describe that artifact against this frozen gold set.

Four identifiers are named because four things can independently change the answer. A network-pharmacology result produced in March and reproduced in September is not the same experiment if the underlying database moved, even if every line of the system's code is identical. A single project-wide version number would hide which axis moved — it would report that "the version changed" when what changed was that HERB gained forty thousand edges.

The claim is also bounded in the other direction. This benchmark measures whether an artifact earned the right to be read as a scientific statement. It does not measure whether the claims inside it are true, whether the study design was good, or whether the clinical advice is sound. Those are for human reviewers, and pretending a scalar can decide them is exactly the failure this project is built against.

2. The Season is the unit of comparison

Scores are comparable only within a Season. A Season is cut from a Stable Registry revision, frozen on a published date, and thereafter immutable — a monthly skill update can never modify it.

Comparing rows across Seasons is comparing two different examinations. Where the leaderboard shows a previous Season, it does so as a reference point, not as a ranking that can be merged with the current one.

3. The six tracks

Each track has twenty cases and its own scorer. The eight dimensions are shared, but what a dimension means is fixed by the track: Task Success on TCM-Entity is entity accuracy and false-merge rate; on TCM-TrialAudit it is per-instrument checklist coverage.

The tracks are separate because the failure modes are separate, and averaging them into one number before you have seen them apart would hide the thing you need to see. A system can be excellent at retrieval and dangerous at safety; a single figure would report it as mediocre at both.

5. Aggregation

The aggregate is a weighted geometric mean of the eight dimension scores, with a floor eps applied to each term.

Why a geometric mean

A geometric mean punishes imbalance. Under an arithmetic mean, a system that is strong on six dimensions can buy its way out of being weak on the other two: a collapse from 0.80 to 0.20 on Safety and Abstention costs the same as a fall from 0.80 to 0.60 twice. Under a geometric mean it does not, because what enters the sum is the logarithm, and a small value dominates it.

That is the intended behaviour. The dimensions are not substitutes for one another. A system that cannot ground its evidence is not half-credited for being fast, and a system that misses safety signals does not recover the loss with a good citation list. If the dimensions were substitutable, the aggregate would be a reasonable summary; because they are not, an aggregate that treats them as substitutable is a misleading one.

Why a floor

A bare geometric mean has a discontinuity at zero: one dimension scoring exactly 0 makes the whole aggregate 0, which converts a single bad axis into a total loss and destroys the information in the other seven. The floor keeps that from happening. It is not a rescue: a genuinely failing run is stopped by a hard gate, and gates are applied independently of the aggregate. The floor affects the arithmetic of the summary, and nothing about who is on which board.

Latency and Cost

Latency and Cost are not naturally 0–1 quantities, so each is mapped through a reference band fixed when the Season is cut, producing a subscore where higher is better — faster and cheaper. The raw value in seconds and US dollars is published next to the subscore on every row and every run page, so the mapping never hides the measurement. The mapping exists so these two axes can enter the same product as the other six without inventing a scale after seeing the results.

6. The hard gates

A gate is not a score contribution and it is not a weighting. It cannot be traded against a high aggregate, and no amount of performance elsewhere buys a way past it. Gates are applied before ranking.

Why gated systems are still shown为什么被门槛拦下的系统仍然显示

A blocked system appears on a clearly labelled Experimental board with its blocking reason attached. It is never silently dropped, and it is never quietly ranked below the trusted rows as if it had merely scored lower. Those are different statements and they deserve different interfaces.

There are two reasons for this. The first is that hiding a failure is a way of lying with a table. A leaderboard that removes its non-compliant entries reports a compliance rate of 100% by construction, which is a property of the filter, not of the field. The second is that a blocked row is genuinely informative: the system that fabricates 1.8% of its citations has an otherwise strong profile, and that combination — strong, fast, and not trustworthy — is precisely what a reader needs to see rather than have withheld.

7. The prediction prohibition七、预测禁令

This is the part of the methodology that matters most, and the part most likely to be misunderstood as a mere formatting rule. It is not.

Docking, network inference, target prediction, pathway enrichment and molecular dynamics are not observations of the world. They are outputs of a model. Placing them on the same evidence ladder as an assay, a trial or a systematic review is a category error, and it is the category error that produces most of the overclaiming in this field: a predicted binding score is rendered as a finding, the finding is aggregated into a network, the network is summarised as a mechanism, and the mechanism is stated as a reason to treat a patient.

The rule the benchmark enforces is therefore about claim kinds, not about evidence quality:

  • A mechanism claim may rest on computational prediction. It is permitted, and it is reported with a caveat naming the prediction it rests on.
  • A clinical claim may not. If a clinical claim is supported only by predictive designs, it is refused with CLM004 at the claim layer and ART106 at the artifact layer, and the run is gated to the Experimental board.

Because the rule is about claim kinds, the same evidence base can support one claim and not another inside the same artifact. A run that predicts a binding mode may say the predicted pose is sterically plausible and may not say the formula is effective for this indication in humans. Both statements rest on the identical docking output. Only one of them is a claim the evidence can carry.

Prediction-as-fact is counted separately from ordinary overclaiming, and never averaged into a general overclaim rate. The two failures call for different responses — one is a calibration problem, the other is a category problem — and collapsing them into a single number would lose exactly the distinction this project exists to report. The claim layer and the artifact layer each keep their own vocabulary (CLM004 and ART106) and the artifact layer maps between them, so a published report has one stable set of strings while the benchmark retains both counts.

Refusing a claim does not cost the run a point. The refused claim is excluded from scoring rather than scored as wrong. This is deliberate: the incentive should point at honesty, not at hedging. A run that declines to state a clinical conclusion it cannot support should not be ranked below one that states it anyway — that would be a benchmark rewarding the failure it claims to measure.

8. Abstention is scored, not penalised八、弃权计分，而非受罚

Safety and Abstention treats a refusal as an outcome. Abstaining with a stated reason scores above answering without evidence, and a refusal carries a code rather than a blank. A system that answers everything cannot score well on this dimension, because risk recall is measured on cases where the correct behaviour includes declining.

The corresponding gate is the sharpest one here: a severe false negative — a missed serious interaction or contraindication that the gold set flags — blocks the trusted board. The Season tolerance is expressed as a rate so that the failure is measurable and reportable, not so that it can be averaged away. In this Season the published tolerance is zero cases.

9. Versioning

Four axes version independently. Every citable result names all four, and a run whose four versions are not all resolvable in the published registries is not rendered on the leaderboard at all.

The four independently versioned axes
AxisChanges whenWhy it is separate
Runtime the executing code changes A bug fix in the kernel is not a change in what the benchmark asks.
Skill a promotion is approved Adding a skill must not force a runtime release; a promotion must not silently alter a frozen Season.
Source来源 a connector's snapshot moves External databases change without telling us. A moved database is a different experiment.
Benchmark a Season is cut The yardstick must not move while results are being compared against it.

External sources are pinned by content, not by date: a source card records a snapshot hash over the normalised response of every declared operation, and connector results carry that hash. "HERB 2.0" is not a version. The hash is. Drift then surfaces as a changed source hash rather than as an unexplained change in output, which is the difference between a discrepancy you can diagnose and one you can only argue about.

The string a leaderboard row and a paper table cite is the composite of all four, not any individual version.

10. Conflict of interest

The party that builds a system must not be the party that scores it, and the party that scores it must not be able to change the score afterwards.

  • Scoring is separated from submission. Evaluation runs in the Runner under a frozen policy snapshot. Submitters do not run the scorer, choose the cases, or see the held-out labels.
  • Nothing on this site can alter a result. The Arena renders published bundles and holds no write path to any registry. A published number cannot be edited by editing the website that displays it, and compromising the website does not compromise a result. This is an architectural property, not a policy — see docs/adr/0004-arena-read-only-static-first.md.
  • Promotion decisions are recorded, attributed and published. A skill enters the Stable Registry only through a PromotionDecision naming the reviewer and the date. There is no automatic path and no popularity threshold. The decision and its identifier are shown on the skill registry.
  • Conflicts are declared. A reviewer with an interest in a submitted system recuses; a system submitted by a maintainer of this benchmark is labelled as such on its row and is reviewed by someone else.
  • The gold answers stay hidden. Cases and labels are not published, so a system cannot be tuned against the set it is scored on. The scorers, the schema and the data card are published so that the scoring itself can be audited.

11. Reproducibility policy十一、可复现性政策

A result is published only if a third party holding the four version axes can re-derive it. Concretely, that requires the run to satisfy all of the following, and each is checked mechanically rather than asserted:

  1. All four axes declared. A composite version missing any axis fails validation (ART108).
  2. Every output hashed. A declared output file with no content hash fails validation (ART107); a file you cannot re-check is a file you cannot trust.
  3. Every source pinned. A publishable claim resting on a source that is not pinned to a snapshot (ART102) or has no identified licence (ART103) fails validation.
  4. Every quote located. A supporting quote that cannot be located in its source is refused (CLM010). A paraphrase presented as a quotation is not a lesser sin than a fabricated citation, only a quieter one.
  5. Re-execution reproduces the hashes. The Reproducibility dimension reports the outcome of an actual re-run within the Season tolerance, not a promise that one is possible.
  6. Limitations stated. An artifact declaring no limitations fails validation (ART110). An artifact that says nothing about its own scope has not earned the right to be read as a scientific statement.

The validator itself is a pure function over a finished artifact: it opens no sockets, reads no clock and consults no registry. The same artifact therefore produces the same verdict today and in five years, which is what makes a published result re-checkable by a reviewer who was not there when it ran.

12. Known limitations of these methods十二、本方法已知的局限

  • Twenty cases per track is a small sample. Each row publishes a 95% interval on the aggregate for that reason. Small differences between adjacent ranks are not meaningful, and the leaderboard does not claim they are.
  • Checklist coverage is not quality. TCM-TrialAudit measures whether a report addresses the items STRICTA, SPIRIT-TCM and CONSORT-CHM ask for. A report can address every item and still describe a bad trial.
  • Latency and cost are environment-dependent. Their subscores are comparable within a Season and within a submission type, and are weak evidence across types — a container's cold start is not the same cost as a remote API call.
  • The track set is a choice. Six tracks were selected because they are the failure modes with the clearest published instruments behind them. That is an argument, not a proof of completeness.

These limitations are stated here for the same reason the benchmark requires runs to state theirs. An evaluation surface that does not describe its own scope is asking for a kind of trust it has not earned.

TCMScience Arena. A read-only evaluation surface. It renders published result bundles; it never computes a score, never re-ranks, and never writes a registry.

Static HTML, one stylesheet, one script. No server, no database, no cookies, no tracking.
